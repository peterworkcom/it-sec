# API Provider Assessment

> When you use an AI model through an **API** (like OpenAI or Anthropic), there is **no file** on your computer.

- So the usual scanning tools **do not work**.
- You have to **trust the provider**.
- You **cannot check** their training data, their changes, or their servers.
- The provider might **change the model** without telling you.

**System prompts** copied from public websites are also a risk. Once you use them, they become part of your supply chain.

## Defence 1: Check the provider first

Before you use a provider, look at these things:

- **Data handling.** Can you stop them using your data for training?
- **Model versions.** Do they warn you before changing the model?
- **Security certificates.** Do they have things like SOC 2 or ISO 27001?
- **Incident response.** Do they have a security contact?
- **Transparency.** Do they explain how the model works?

**Warning sign:** no clear answers, or changes made without telling you.

## Defence 2: Watch how the model behaves

You cannot see inside the model. So **watch what it says** instead.

- Ask the **same test questions** on a regular schedule.
- Look for **big changes** in the answers.
- Track **accuracy**, **format**, and how often it **refuses**.
- Watch for changes in **speed** or **errors**.

**Key point:** you cannot check the file, so you check the behaviour.

## Defence 3: Control your system prompts

Treat system prompts **like code**.

- Keep them in **version control**.
- **Review** any changes.
- **Test** changes before you use them for real.

## Defence 4: Test in a safe space first

Test the model in an **isolated sandbox** before using it for real.

There are **4 steps**:

1. **Load it safely.** It must work in a closed-off space.
2. **Ask fixed questions.** The answers must be correct.
3. **Try to trick it.** Its safety rules must hold.
4. **Compare it.** It must behave like your current model.

**Pass all 4** -> use it.
**Fail any** -> reject it.

**Important:** do not just trust public test scores. A model can be built to pass them but still have hidden problems. **Your own tests** are the ones to trust.
